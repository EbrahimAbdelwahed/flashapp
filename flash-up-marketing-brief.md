# Flash Up — Marketing Brief

## Purpose

This document is the strategic source of truth for creating marketing copy with
Claude/Fable. It defines who the product is for, which problem it solves, and
which promises the copy may make. It is not a library of finished ads.

## Product in one sentence

Flash Up is an iOS flashcard app that lets students import cards they already
generated with ChatGPT, then start reviewing immediately without creating an
account or paying a recurring subscription.

## Primary audience

### Core segment

University students who:

- use an iPhone and are mainly in the first years of university;
- are still refining their study method;
- already use ChatGPT, or are willing to use it, to turn course material into
  flashcards;
- understand that flashcards may help but have not adopted them consistently;
- are discouraged by expensive subscriptions, complex interfaces, setup, or
  rigid platforms that insist on generating the cards themselves;
- want a quick, low-risk way to try a better study workflow.

### Audience state of mind

They are not looking for the most configurable flashcard system. They want to
study with less friction. Their likely thoughts are:

- “I know flashcards could help, but setting everything up takes too long.”
- “ChatGPT can already generate the cards, so why should I pay every month?”
- “I do not know whether this method will work for me, so I do not want another
  subscription.”
- “I want to import what I already have and start studying.”

### Not the initial audience

- advanced Anki users who value deep customization and plugins;
- users looking primarily for an AI content-generation platform;
- Android-first users;
- institutions seeking classroom administration or analytics.

## Jobs to be done

### Functional job

Move from flashcards generated in ChatGPT to an active review session in a few
seconds, with spaced repetition, backup, and synchronization already handled.

### Emotional job

Feel that starting a serious study method is easy, affordable, and reversible:
the student can try it without committing to a complicated system or another
monthly expense.

### Social job

Use a modern study workflow that can be explained and shared easily with
friends: generate, import, and study.

## Problem hierarchy

1. **Recurring cost:** many study tools turn a simple reviewing need into a
   monthly or annual payment.
2. **Setup friction:** accounts, settings, plugins, and unfamiliar workflows
   delay the moment when studying begins.
3. **Import friction:** some products want to generate the cards themselves
   instead of accepting cards the student already made elsewhere.
4. **Method uncertainty:** students who are still experimenting do not want a
   large financial or cognitive commitment.

## Positioning

### Category

A simple iOS flashcard reviewer for cards created elsewhere, especially with
ChatGPT.

### Competitive frame

Do not position Flash Up as “better than Anki.” Anki is powerful and respected
by its users. The opponent is the combination of recurring subscriptions and
unnecessary complexity.

### Positioning statement

For university students with an iPhone who want to try flashcards without
adopting another complicated subscription service, Flash Up turns cards
generated with ChatGPT into a synchronized review session in seconds. Import
once, pay once, and study.

## Value proposition

### Primary promise

Import the flashcards you already have and start studying immediately.

### Supporting promises

- one small, one-time purchase;
- no proprietary account or password;
- no recurring subscription for the core experience;
- backup and synchronization across supported Apple devices;
- spaced repetition without configuration overhead;
- no advertising inside the app.

### Message hierarchy

1. **Import the cards you already generated.**
2. **Open the app and start studying.**
3. **Pay once instead of adding another subscription.**
4. **Synchronization and backup happen without a separate account.**

“No advertising” is a supporting reassurance, not the lead message.

## Message territory

### Strategic idea

**Not everything needs to be a subscription.**

This expresses the product’s commercial model and the student-friendly stance
behind it. It should support the practical promise, not replace it.

### Product shorthand

**ChatGPT → import → study.**

This is the clearest demonstration of the workflow and should anchor product
videos, landing-page explanations, and App Store visuals.

### Transformation

Before: cards exist in a chat or file, but beginning a real review habit feels
expensive or complicated.

After: the cards are imported, synchronized, and ready for review within
seconds.

## Copy principles

- Begin with a problem or recognizable student moment, not with “I built an
  app.”
- Sell the outcome—starting to study—not the software itself.
- Use concrete language and show the workflow visually.
- Sound direct, calm, and student-friendly; avoid corporate or educational
  jargon.
- Treat affordability as dignity and accessibility, not as “cheapness.”
- Avoid attacking named competitors or claiming that serious users are wrong.
- Avoid vague AI claims: ChatGPT generates the source material; Flash Up helps
  import and review it.
- Do not promise features, platforms, or automation that the product does not
  yet support.

## Content strategy

### Primary channels

- TikTok
- Instagram Reels
- short-form video suitable for reuse on similar platforms

### Production model

Use a small set of repeatable formats with many variations rather than making
every video from scratch. Scripts, hooks, captions, and descriptions can be
generated in batches; the final content should still vary in problem, example,
course context, and proof.

### Strongest visual format

Short screen recordings that demonstrate:

1. generating or formatting flashcards in ChatGPT;
2. exporting or preparing the CSV;
3. importing it into Flash Up;
4. beginning a review session.

The product should be understood from the screen even before the viewer hears
the full explanation.

### Initial content pillars

- the cost and fatigue of recurring study subscriptions;
- common mistakes when using ChatGPT for studying;
- turning existing notes or ChatGPT output into a review habit;
- simple study-method education for early university students;
- fast, concrete demonstrations of the import-to-review workflow.

## Claims requiring product verification

Copy must not publish these claims until the implementation confirms them:

- exact import duration, such as “ten seconds”;
- exact supported CSV format and error handling;
- which Apple devices synchronize;
- offline behavior and conflict resolution;
- whether no account is literally true in every iCloud state;
- backup and recovery behavior;
- exact one-time price;
- whether sharing decks with friends is part of the first release;
- “no advertising, ever” as a permanent business commitment.

## Open strategic decisions

- final product name and spelling;
- exact launch price and whether a trial is needed;
- countries and languages for the first launch;
- which university subjects should provide the first examples;
- whether the initial target is broad university study or a narrower vertical
  such as medicine;
- whether sharing with friends belongs in the launch story;
- which proof points can be demonstrated honestly at launch;
- the desired brand personality and visual identity.

## Prompt contract for Claude/Fable

When using this brief to generate copy:

1. treat the audience, positioning, and message hierarchy as constraints;
2. distinguish verified product claims from concepts still awaiting a decision;
3. generate variations within the defined message territory instead of changing
   the product strategy;
4. lead with a student problem, then show the workflow, then present the
   one-time-purchase model;
5. avoid inventing features, testimonials, performance figures, or competitor
   pricing;
6. label exploratory copy clearly so it cannot be mistaken for approved claims.
